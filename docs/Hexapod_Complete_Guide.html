Project Overview

Chipo — The Student-Friendly Hexapod

Chipo is the budget-friendly variant of the MakeYourPet hexapod family, designed for students and hobbyists. It uses MG996R servos instead of the expensive coreless metal-gear DS3235 servos used in the premium "Chica" version. All mechanical parts are 3D printed in PLA/PETG.

Chipo (Student Version)
  • 18x MG996R servos (metal gear)
  • Femur length: 60mm
  • Tibia length: 104mm
  • Files in hexapod-main/Chipo/
  • Target budget: PKR 40,000–48,000
Chica (Premium Version)
  • 18x DS3235 / DSSERVO coreless servos
  • Femur length: 80mm
  • Tibia length: 134mm
  • Budget: PKR 80,000–120,000+
  • NOT recommended for students
Degrees of Freedom

6 legs × 3 joints = 18 DOF total. Each leg has: Coxa (hip rotation), Femur (up/down), Tibia (knee bend). This is a true hexapod with full inverse kinematics support.

Gait Modes
  • STANDARD — Normal walking gait
  • RACE — Fast movement
  • OFFROAD — Uneven terrain
  • CUSTOM — User-defined
  • QUADRUPED — 4-leg mode
  • BLOCK — Robot stays still
Purpose (For Defense)

"Autonomous Bio-Inspired Hexapod for Hazardous Terrain Reconnaissance and Disaster Search Operations" — uses terrain-adaptive touch sensors, 6-legged stability for rubble/uneven ground, and AI-powered vision via mounted smartphone.

Electronics & Wiring Details

Pimoroni Servo2040 Board
  • MCU: RP2040 dual-core ARM Cortex-M0+
  • 18 dedicated PWM servo channels (P0–P17)
  • Current monitoring per channel (via INA219)
  • Screw terminal: 10A continuous max, reverse polarity protected
  • Onboard LED bar (6 LEDs) for status
  • USB-C for programming AND phone connection
  • 6 additional GPIO (P18–P23) — used for touch sensors
  • Relay output: P26 (High-Active)
  • Voltage monitoring pins available
CRITICAL MOD REQUIRED: You MUST physically cut the "Separate USB and Ext. Power" trace on the BACK of the Servo2040 board before connecting a battery voltage above 5V. Otherwise you risk damaging the RP2040 chip or your phone.

Touch Sensor & Protection Configuration
Touch Sensors (P18–P23)
  • P18 = TS_L1 (Left Front leg tip)
  • P19 = TS_L2 (Left Mid leg tip)
  • P20 = TS_L3 (Left Rear leg tip)
  • P21 = TS_R1 (Right Front leg tip)
  • P22 = TS_R2 (Right Mid leg tip)
  • P23 = TS_R3 (Right Rear leg tip)
  • Logic: High-Active (pressed = HIGH)
Protection Thresholds (from chipo-config)
  • Voltage WARNING: 5.4V (per cell: 2.7V)
  • Voltage CUTOFF: 5.0V (per cell: 2.5V)
  • Current WARNING: 5.0A total
  • Current CUTOFF: 7.0A total
  • Relay Pin: P26 (High = servos ENABLED)
  • PWM Range: ~980–2050 microseconds

Firmware — How to Flash Servo2040

FYP Defense Warning: The pre-compiled .uf2 firmware is made by Eddie Carrera (not you). For FYP, you CANNOT submit this as your own work. Solution: Use the Eddie firmware to test hardware first, then write your own MicroPython IK firmware. The chassis + physical build is your work; the software must be custom.

Firmware Download Link (Direct)

https://github.com/EddieCarrera/chica-servo2040-simpleDriver/releases/download/v0.0.1/chica-servo2040_release.uf2

Save this file as chica-servo2040_release.uf2 on your computer. This is the only released version (v0.0.1).

1

Cut the PCB Trace FIRST

On the back of the Servo2040, locate the "Separate USB and Ext. Power" jumper trace. Use a sharp hobby knife or scalpel to CUT this trace. This prevents 7.4V battery from backfeeding into USB port and destroying the chip or your phone. DO THIS BEFORE connecting any battery.

2

Enter Bootloader Mode

Plug USB-C cable into your computer. Then: Hold the "BOOT/USER" button down, press and release the RESET button simultaneously, then release BOOT button. The board will appear as a removable USB drive on your computer (like a USB flash drive).

3

Copy the .uf2 File

Simply drag and drop the chica-servo2040_release.uf2 file onto the RP2040 drive that appeared. The board will automatically reboot and the LED bar will light up to confirm firmware is running.

4

Install Chica Server App on Phone

On your Android phone, install "Chica Server" from Google Play Store. Enable USB Debugging on the phone. Connect phone to Servo2040 via USB-C data cable. Open Chica Server app and grant USB permissions.

5

Load Config File

Upload the chipo-config-2040.txt from the GitHub repo to the Chica Server app. This file contains all servo PWM calibration values, pin assignments, and protection thresholds. Without this, servos will not move correctly.

6

Control with Chica Client

On a second phone (or same phone with second app), install "Chica Client". This connects to the Chica Server over WiFi (same network) and provides joystick controls for the robot.

Assembly Sequence (Step-by-Step)

Print order: Print one leg first to test fit before printing all 6. STL files in hexapod-main/Chipo/ for Chipo version. Use the highest-numbered file variant (latest version).
1

3D Print All Parts

  • coxa-996.stl — 6 pieces (one per leg)
  • femur-996.stl — 6 pieces
  • tibia-996.stl — 6 pieces
  • tip.stl — 6 pieces (foot tip)
  • ring.stl — 6 pieces (femur ring)
  • idler.stl — 6–12 pieces
  • rod.stl — 6 pieces (tibia rod)
  • ms-holder.stl — 6 pieces (microswitch holder)
  • limitter.stl — 6 pieces (travel limiter)
  • frame.stl — 1 piece (main body)
  • top-cover4.stl — 1 piece (use highest number)
  • bottom-cover2.stl — 1 piece
  • battery-bar.stl — 1 piece
  • phone-bar.stl — 1 piece
  • shield.stl — 1 piece

Print settings: 0.2mm layer height, 4 perimeters, 20–30% infill (gyroid or honeycomb).

2

Servo Zeroing (CRITICAL — Do Before Assembly)

Before inserting any servo into a leg, center each servo to 90° (1500ms pulse). Connect each servo individually to a servo tester or microcontroller. Set to center position THEN attach the servo horn. If you skip this, you cannot calibrate later without disassembly.

3

Build One Leg Assembly

  • Coxa servo mounts into coxa bracket (M3 screws)
  • Femur servo mounts into femur bracket
  • Tibia servo drives tibia via the rod linkage
  • Tip clips onto tibia end
  • Microswitch press-fits into ms-holder at tip
  • Test range of motion before continuing to next leg
4

Build All 6 Legs

Repeat leg assembly x6. Keep left and right legs mirror-imaged. Attach coxa assemblies to the main frame (hexagonal body). Use M3 x 10mm or M3 x 12mm screws depending on frame thickness.

5

Wire All 18 Servos

Route servo wires through frame channels to the center. Follow the wiring diagram exactly. Use the pin mapping from Section 5: L11=P0, L12=P1... etc. Bundle wires with zip ties to keep neat. Label each connector before plugging in.

6

Mount Servo2040 + Relay

Mount Servo2040 to bottom cover plate. Mount relay module nearby. Connect relay: control signal to P26, relay COM to battery+, relay NO to Servo2040 power terminal. This allows firmware to kill servo power in emergencies.

7

Connect Battery, Switch, and Touch Sensors

Wire battery through main power switch, then through relay to Servo2040 screw terminal. Connect 6 touch sensors to P18–P23. Install battery-bar for LiPo, phone-bar for Android phone. Close top/bottom covers.

8

Flash Firmware and Calibrate

Flash the .uf2 firmware (see Section 6). Load chipo-config-2040.txt. Test each servo individually via the app. Fine-tune PWM values in config file until all joints are properly centered. This is the most time-consuming step (2–5 hours).

def inverse_kinematics(x, y, z):
    """3DOF IK per leg — returns [coxa_angle, femur_angle, tibia_angle]"""
    import math
    coxa_angle = math.degrees(math.atan2(y, x))
    l = math.sqrt(x**2 + y**2)  # horizontal distance
    d = math.sqrt(l**2 + z**2)  # 3D distance
    a1 = math.degrees(math.atan2(-z, l))
    a2 = math.degrees(math.acos((FEMUR**2 + d**2 - TIBIA**2) / (2 * FEMUR * d)))
    femur_angle = a1 + a2
    tibia_angle = math.degrees(math.acos((FEMUR**2 + TIBIA**2 - d**2) / (2 * FEMUR * TIBIA)))
    return coxa_angle, femur_angle, tibia_angle - 180

Engineering Challenges & Solutions

1. High Power Consumption

Challenge: 18 motors drain the battery quickly (20-30 min max).

Solution: Implement "Sleep Mode" in software to drop chassis to the ground and cut relay power to servos when idle. Use high C-rating (40C+) 6200mAh LiPo.

2. Complex Kinematics Math

Challenge: 18-DOF Inverse Kinematics requires heavy trigonometry and matrices.

Solution: Offload complex math to the Android smartphone processor. Use Law of Cosines for geometric 3-DOF IK instead of matrix multiplication. Send final angles to RP2040 via Serial.

3. Hardware Play & Vibration

Challenge: MG996R servos have mechanical play causing jitter.

Solution: Upgrade to Aluminium (25T) metal servo horns. Implement LERP (Linear Interpolation) in code for smooth angle transitions.

4. Complex Wire Management

Challenge: Routing 18 servo cables causes tangles and interference.

Solution: Label all connectors BEFORE routing. Use spiral wire wrap to combine 3 servo wires per leg into 1 thick cable. Use zip ties for inner chassis routing.

FYP Defense Preparation

Expected Examiner Questions
  • Q: "What is the purpose of this robot?"
    A: Autonomous bio-inspired hexapod for hazardous terrain navigation, disaster search & rescue operations in environments too dangerous for humans or wheeled robots.
  • Q: "Why 6 legs and not wheels?"
    A: Hexapod locomotion provides 100% static stability at all times (3 legs always on ground), can navigate rubble, stairs, and uneven terrain impossible for wheeled or tracked robots. Bio-inspired from insects.
  • Q: "What did YOU code?"
    A: Custom MicroPython IK firmware on RP2040, tripod gait algorithm, touch sensor terrain adaptation, AI voice assistant integration using Gemini API, real-time vision processing pipeline on Android.
  • Q: "What is Inverse Kinematics?"
    A: Mathematical method to calculate required joint angles (coxa, femur, tibia) to achieve a desired foot position in 3D space. We use 3-DOF IK per leg with geometric solver.
  • Q: "What is the cost?"
    A: Student version: PKR 42,000–58,000. Cost-optimized using MG996R servos instead of premium coreless servos, 3D printed chassis instead of aluminum CNC.
