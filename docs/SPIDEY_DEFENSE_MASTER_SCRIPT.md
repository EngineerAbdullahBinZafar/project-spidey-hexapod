> - Then, projecting the leg into the 2D sagittal plane, we solve the Femur and Tibia angles using the **Law of Cosines**.  
> The result? **Deterministic, guaranteed convergence in under 450 microseconds per iteration**. Exactly six legs, three equations per leg, yielding zero tracking latency."*

## 🛡️ Evaluator Q&A Battlecards (Doctor-Level Defense Answers)

### Question 1: *"Why did you use Closed-Form IK instead of Numerical/Jacobian IK?"*
* **Answer by Junaid Khan**:  
  *"Sir, Numerical Jacobian methods require matrix inversion at every step ($J^{-1}$ or $J^T$), which on an embedded Cortex-M0+ consumes excessive clock cycles and risks mathematical singularities where the matrix becomes non-invertible. Our Closed-Form analytical trigonometry is deterministic, runs in under 450 microseconds, and mathematically cannot diverge or get stuck in local minima."*

### Question 2: *"Why 6 legs instead of 4 (quadruped) or 8 (octopod)?"*
* **Answer by Abdullah Bin Zafar**:  
  *"Sir, a quadruped (like Boston Dynamics Spot) requires continuous dynamic balance and active inertial stabilization because lifting two legs reduces support to an unstable line. An octopod adds 6 more servos, increasing mass and power draw unnecessarily. Six legs represent the optimal golden ratio of biology: static stability during motion with minimal actuator complexity via the alternating tripod."*

### Question 3: *"How do you handle motor overheating or high current stall during rubble climbing?"*
* **Answer by Fatima Ahmed**:  
  *"Sir, our 20A UBEC has thermal shutdown and over-current protection. Furthermore, the RP2040 firmware implements a torque-limiting duty cycle: if a leg fails to reach its target Z-height within a prescribed window (indicating a physical obstacle or jam), the IK engine aborts the step and retries with a higher leg lift trajectory rather than pulling stall current."*
